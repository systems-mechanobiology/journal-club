# Contributing

Use English throughout this repository and its GitHub conversations.

## Recommend a paper

1. Check the catalog and existing discussions for its DOI.
2. If a paper discussion already exists, add your question there.
3. Otherwise, open a **Paper Discussions** post using the form. Use the title `[Journal | Year] Full paper title`.
4. Provide the citation, DOI or publisher link, primary topic, and a short reason to read it. A full analysis is optional.
5. Add a catalog entry by pull request, or ask a maintainer to add it. Link to the actual discussion after it exists.

Choose one primary topic for navigation. Add secondary topic labels when useful. Apply `collection:time-series` only when temporal data or dynamics are substantively relevant; a future outcome alone is not sufficient.

Use the online publication year consistently. A later issue year can be noted separately. Distinguish original research, reviews, perspectives, news, and corrections.

## Discuss a paper

Use one top-level comment per question or argument; use replies for that thread. Cite the relevant figure, table, section, or supplement whenever possible.

Separate what the authors claim, what the evidence shows, and your interpretation. Discuss ideas respectfully, preserve substantive disagreement, and state uncertainty. A journal's acceptance decision is not evidence that every claim is correct.

Optional comment format:

- **Observation or question:**
- **Evidence location:**
- **Interpretation or alternative:**
- **What would resolve this?**

## Run a journal club session

- The presenter opens a **Journal Club** post with a date, time zone, and links to existing paper discussions.
- Before the meeting, members add a question or observation to the relevant paper discussion.
- During the meeting, focus on the problem, contribution, decisive evidence, and remaining uncertainties.
- Afterwards, a recorder adds `Session summary - YYYY-MM-DD` to each paper discussion and links those comments from the session post.
- Capture takeaways, disagreements, open questions, and follow-up issues. Do not fabricate consensus or attribute views to absent members.
- Change `reading:scheduled` to `reading:discussed` after the session; keep the paper discussion open.

## Use issues for actionable work

Open an issue when a discussion produces a specific task: verify a split, check a cited claim, correct metadata, or prepare a comparison. Link the originating discussion and define what completion means. Paper critiques and ordinary questions belong in Discussions.

## Use pull requests for lasting changes

Propose catalog updates, corrected citations, topic navigation, or cross-paper synthesis through a PR. Link the evidence and originating conversations. A maintainer reviews the change. New synthesis should identify supporting papers and counterexamples.

Keep one canonical catalog entry per DOI and one main paper discussion. Topic pages and collections are navigational views of the catalog, not duplicate reviews.

## Sources

Link to the publisher, DOI, accessible author manuscript, code, or data. Summarize in your own words. This repository stores reading context and discussion rather than a bulk PDF collection.
